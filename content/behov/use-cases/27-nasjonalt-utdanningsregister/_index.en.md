## Case description – National education register

### Who submitted this as a case the SAMT-BU project should address?

Sikt, based on ongoing work on the education register and the need to connect it to coherent services across educational levels. The case is closely linked to the merger of Vilbli and [utdanning.no](http://utdanning.no), and to the long-term goal of a shared, authoritative register for all education information in Norway.

The inter-agency group for digital transformation in the education sector – consisting of the top executives of the agencies under the Ministry of Education and Research – supports the initiative. One of four areas of action in the group's strategy is precisely national registers and data sharing.

### Brief description of the case (what is this / what does it concern?)

The case is part of the work to establish a holistic national infrastructure for the collection and sharing of data on education and competence in Norway. The infrastructure consists of three components:

1. National education register: A register in which all formal and non-formal education at all levels will be registered. Sikt has received funding from the Ministry of Education and Research to start developing the register for vocational colleges, universities and university colleges. For the period 2023–25, NOK 13.95 million has been allocated for this. The register must be further developed to cover the entire educational pathway from primary and lower secondary school to higher education.

2. National competence register: A further development of the National Certificate Database (NVB) into a register in which all diplomas, transcripts, course certificates, certifications, micro-credentials, decisions on recognition of foreign education and documented prior learning are stored long-term. Each citizen will have all their competence documented in one place, and can view and share it through the Diploma Portal (Vitnemålsportalen).

3. National service for identifying persons in education: A service that handles the challenges of identifying foreign students and staff, and enables login across organisations throughout the educational pathway.

These three registers are closely interconnected and are necessary to exploit the potential. For example, when there are digital diplomas, recipients must be able to verify them digitally. The education register is planned to be used to verify accreditation status for both the educational institution and the programme.

Pilot 4 covers item 2, the competence certificate register. This case is about doing the equivalent for item 1, the national education register.

Finland has shown that this is possible: in 2018 the Finnish National Agency for Education (Utbildningsstyrelsen) established KOSKI – a national register with data from all levels of the education system. The data is used by citizens, businesses and authorities in a wide range of contexts. Norway should build a corresponding infrastructure.

The case can be carried out either as a stand-alone pilot or as an integrated part of another pilot in the SAMT-BU project.

The education register is a national register that will gather information about accredited programmes and educational institutions in Norway. The register is to be a shared and authoritative source of data on education in Norwegian higher education and vocational college education.

The register will gather information that is currently spread across several systems and actors. Each programme and each educational institution will be given a unique digital identity that can be used across systems and services. This will make it easier to refer to, share and reuse information about programmes in different contexts.

The education register will store basic information about programmes, educational institutions and accreditations securely over time. Changes to programmes and organisations will be documented, so that it is also possible to verify historical information.

The register is to support both national and international needs for sharing education data. The information can be used by universities, university colleges, vocational colleges, public authorities and other services working with education, statistics and documentation of competence.

In the long term, the education register will contribute to better data quality, more efficient information sharing and the development of new digital services related to education and competence.

The merger of Vilbli and [utdanning.no](http://utdanning.no) is directly affected by this work. Both services currently provide information about programmes, but maintain their own databases with no connection to a shared authoritative register. In the long term, they should retrieve their information directly from the education register.

Several of the other cases submitted to SAMT-BU will also be affected by this register, and it will help simplify the data flow between different systems.

### What problem does the case address / what is not working well today?

Data about programmes is spread across many actors, and they have no unambiguous identification. Norway therefore lacks a systematic overview of which programmes and courses are offered at any given time at all levels. There is no national overview that can be used in digital services.

### Where do breakdowns in information flow or responsibility occur?

Breakdowns in information flow and responsibility occur in particular at the following points:

Between educational levels: Information about programmes in primary and lower secondary education, upper secondary and higher education is managed by different actors and systems without shared coordination. The register is currently established only for the university and university college sector, and is prepared for the vocational college sector.

Between the register and the information services: Services such as Vilbli and [utdanning.no](http://utdanning.no) maintain their own databases and do not retrieve information from a shared authoritative register, which creates a risk of inconsistency and duplicated work.

In the legal basis: The legal basis for establishing, collecting data for and managing all the registers must be investigated. The register must have the necessary legal authority.

### Who are the primary actors affected

End users:

Citizens seeking information about educational provision and courses at all levels. Pupils and students who need an overview of their own results and qualifications. Employees who want to document and share their competence throughout their working career. Employers who need to verify the competence and qualifications of candidates and employees.

Service staff:

Staff at universities and university colleges responsible for the course catalogue and education information. Staff in county authorities responsible for the range of programmes offered in upper secondary education. Editors and content providers for Vilbli and [utdanning.no](http://utdanning.no). Caseworkers at Lånekassen (the State Educational Loan Fund).

Specialist system suppliers:

Suppliers of student administration systems (including the Common Student System – FS). The Norwegian Directorate for Education and Training, which draws up and decides which subjects are to be taught in primary, lower secondary and upper secondary education. Suppliers of school administration systems for upper secondary and primary and lower secondary school. Suppliers who want to develop services based on data from the registers.

Governing actors:

The Ministry of Education and Research – overall responsibility for the education sector and governance frameworks, recipient of Sikt's investment proposal for 2027. The Norwegian Directorate for Education and Training (Udir) – professional directorate for primary, lower secondary and upper secondary education. The Norwegian Directorate for Higher Education and Skills (HK-dir) – professional directorate for higher education, The Norwegian Digitalisation Agency (Digdir) – governing actor for digital interoperability, data sharing and shared architecture. NOKUT – accreditation of programmes and educational institutions, Lånekassen – a major user of education data in application processing. Sikt – technically responsible for the education register and administrator of shared services in the education sector. SSB – uses education data for statistics and social planning.

### What are the consequences for the actors under the current situation

For citizens and end users:

Incomplete and fragmented data sources for information about educational provision. Limited access to career guidance tools that can show which competence one lacks to qualify for a job.

For public service providers:

Lånekassen will increase automation of application processing when it receives data from an authoritative source. Many agencies spend time quality-assuring the data, e.g. [Utdanning.no](http://Utdanning.no).

For the sector:

Weakened ability to dimension educational provision: without good data on supply and demand, it is difficult to ensure that educational capacity matches the needs of working life, in both the shorter and longer term (cf. White Paper 14, 2022/23). Failure to meet the EU's interoperability requirements through the Interoperable Europe Act and requirements related to the Single Digital Gateway.

For Sikt:

Uncertain funding for operation and further development threatens the continuity of an already established register. Without a permanent budget adjustment, the established components will quickly become technical debt.

### How are actors affected by the current situation, and what are the possible consequences

The consequences are felt at individual level, service level and societal level.

For the individual citizen, the situation means there is no single place to find information about educational provision and one's own qualifications. Competence is spread across diplomas, course certificates, certifications and assessments of prior learning – with no combined digital overview.

For public services, the lack of shared, quality-assured data means that many spend time quality-assuring the same data. . Agencies such as Lånekassen, the Norwegian Directorate of Health and the Directorate of Integration and Diversity already retrieve data from the Diploma Portal, but would benefit greatly from a more complete data source.

For working life, the consequence is that it is difficult to verify competence quickly and digitally. The Norwegian Construction Federation (Byggenæringens landsforbund), for example, is exploring the possibility of digital HSE cards with information about the courses and certifications required to work on construction sites – access to data in further developed registers will be crucial here.

For statistics and social planning, there is a lack of a complete and up-to-date knowledge base on the population's competence, which is necessary to target measures regionally and nationally and to meet the challenge of having enough labour with the right competence in the future.

The challenges are not about a lack of will, but about structural barriers: historically grown silos, unclarified legal frameworks, the lack of a shared information model and a funding model that is not adapted to the long-term operation of national shared registers.

### What might a desired situation (dream journey) look like?

In a desired situation, a holistic national infrastructure has been established with three interconnected components: an education register covering all levels and all formal and non-formal education, a competence certificate register in which all qualifications are gathered, and a service for unique identification of everyone taking part in education.

Each citizen has easy, digital access to a complete overview of their education and competence throughout their life. The competence can be shared digitally – for example through the EU Digital Identity Wallet – with employers, educational institutions or public agencies as they wish.

Services such as [utdanning.no](http://utdanning.no) and the merged Vilbli retrieve all information about educational provision directly from the education register. The information is always up to date and consistent, regardless of which service the user uses. Career guidance tools can show individuals which competence they lack to qualify for the jobs they want.

Public processes such as application processing at Lånekassen, authorisation of health personnel and processing of applications related to integration are largely automated, because data on education and competence is structured, available and quality-assured in national registers. Lånekassen is in close dialogue with Sikt about adopting the register, in order to be able to automate its own processes

Statisticians and social planners have access to up-to-date, complete data on the population's competence and educational provision, which provides a far better basis for dimensioning education according to the needs of working life – as White Paper 14 (2022/23) requires.

Norway is able to meet the EU's interoperability requirements through the Interoperable Europe Act, and reporting of education data to European registers is simplified and improved. Norwegian infrastructure is on a par with what Finland has built through KOSKI since 2018.

Benefits are realised in four main areas: developing and using competence in society, coherent services and automation, a better basis for statistics and social planning, and strengthened internationalisation.

The following insight work has been carried out

The case builds on a solid foundation of earlier studies and ongoing work:

Sikt's investment proposal to the Ministry of Education and Research for 2027, appendix 2: "Investment proposal: Further development and operation of national registers for education and competence" (ref. 25/00982-1, dated 31.10.2025). This document contains a thorough analysis of problems, measures, benefits and preconditions.

The concept study "Competence platform for lifelong learning" (Report no. 7, 2022, prepared by HK-dir and Sikt). The study describes the need for a digital foundation for the competence platform, in which national registers for education and competence are central building blocks.

The work of the Norwegian Committee on Skill Needs (Kompetansebehovsutvalget) and the Ministry's letter of allocation, which point to the need to further develop the education register so that it includes the entire educational pathway from primary and lower secondary school to higher education.

White Paper 14 (2022/23) Outlook on skills needs in Norway, which emphasises the need for a productive workforce and that the entire education system must be organised and dimensioned so that capacity matches demand.

The new national digitalisation strategy, which clearly points to work on making available national datasets that are important for the public sector and society.

Experience from Finland's establishment of KOSKI in 2018 – a national register and data exchange service for study rights and completed studies at all educational levels.

Ongoing work in the inter-agency group for digital transformation in the education sector, where national registers and data sharing are one of four areas of action.

Experience and learning from pilot 4 in SAMT-BU, which it is desirable to replicate for the education register.

### Which of the project's objectives do we believe the case addresses?

The case addresses all of SAMT-BU's objectives, and in particular:

Shared information models and standardised interfaces: The work on the education register requires an agreed information model across all educational levels and sectors. This is a precondition for data sharing and reuse, and represents one of the most important architecture efforts in the Norwegian public sector.

Reusable solutions and shared solutions: The national registers are in themselves shared solutions that many services and actors can build on – public as well as private. The benefit potential is enormous, but requires the basic infrastructure to be in place.

Simpler user journeys for pupils and students through the learning pathway: With a complete education register and a competence certificate register, pupils, students and employees will have a coherent information interface from primary school throughout their working career. Processes such as applying for student financial support and authorisation can be automated.

Collaboration and innovation across public and private actors: The investment proposal emphasises that the registers will also enable private actors to develop services based on data about education and competence, and that good collaboration with actors who make data available and use it is a precondition for success.

Long-term goals of lifelong learning: A holistic education register and competence certificate register is a direct precondition for realising the ambitions for lifelong learning. The competence certificate register will store all competence documentation throughout a person's life.

Scaling and transfer of the project's learning: The experience from establishing a shared information model and architecture for the education register will be directly transferable to corresponding work in other sectors.
