## Description of the case

### Who submitted the case

The case was submitted by Stavanger municipality (Stine Randa, Educational ICT Adviser, Childhood and Education, [stine.randa@stavanger.kommune.no](mailto:stine.randa@stavanger.kommune.no)) and quality-assured by the area lead for Childhood and Youth in KS Digital.

### Brief description of the case

When national tests and possibly other voluntary mapping assessments from UDIR have been carried out in school, they are available in PAS (the Norwegian Directorate for Education and Training's digital platform used to plan, administer and conduct centrally set exams and tests in primary and lower secondary school and upper secondary education - <https://www.udir.no/eksamen-og-prover/prover/>) for one year, but after that the municipalities must handle them themselves. This also applies to language assessments for children in both kindergarten and school. After one year the data is no longer available to the municipalities, and they are instead referred to a market where there is only one supplier delivering some services. For many municipalities this means they no longer have access to the data. 

This case covers the *analysis needs* of municipalities and schools, not data at individual level. That is dealt with in other cases. 

### What problem does the case address / what is not working well today?

Without easy access to assessment data, it becomes considerably more difficult for municipalities to carry out ongoing analyses, evaluate different measures and follow up groups of pupils. The solution on offer is not satisfactory either.

### Where do breakdowns in information flow or responsibility occur?

The problem arises because UDIR removes the municipalities' access to the data after one year. Whether the reason for this is technical or legal is not known to SAMT-BU.

There is a solution from Conexus (Engage and Insight) that retrieves data from various sources (UDIR and SSB) and combines it into an analysis that municipalities can buy and use further.

### Who are the primary actors affected

- Staff in municipalities and county authorities
- Pupils and parents/guardians

### What are the consequences for the actors under the current situation.

The analyses of educational results carried out by individual municipalities are incomplete and/or unnecessarily costly. Some municipalities do not prioritise carrying out the analyses.

### What might a desired situation (dream journey) look like?

In any case, it must be a goal in itself that the municipality has free access to its own data and can download it, including historical data. If the datasets are released and made available, the municipalities can run analyses themselves, ideally together with other data the municipality holds. The datasets can also be made available to other actors, so that the market can continue to develop ready-made analyses that can be sold to municipalities that want them. 

### The following insight work has been carried out

SAMT-BU is not aware of any insight work carried out specifically in relation to this case. It is assumed, however, that there is a great deal of knowledge about the need both in the municipalities and in the sector more widely.

### Which of the project's objectives do we believe the case addresses?

The case is a good case for SAMT-BU because it addresses part of the core of the project: access to data across levels of government in order to improve public services. Because the case has been scoped to cover analysis needs, we assume there are no insoluble privacy issues. This may make it easier to find good solutions.

\
The case addresses:

- Reusable solutions and shared solutions
- Simpler user journeys for staff, parents/guardians, pupils and students through the learning pathway - from kindergarten to primary and lower secondary school, upper secondary and higher education.
- Collaboration and innovation across public and private actors in the ecosystem, and increased competition in the market.
- Long-term goals of lifelong learning, etc.
- Scaling and transfer of the project's learning and products to other areas and sectors, after piloting in this project.

### A little about implementation

It is absolutely essential to clarify why data is deleted after one year. Whether this has a legal or a technical justification is decisive for what a pilot should work on. A pilot should therefore start by mapping the current situation and why it is the way it is. Ownership of data will be a key concept here. 

Because there is already an actor with a market monopoly on end-user solutions, it is naturally also important to get an overview of what is delivered, what business model they have and whether there are needs that are not covered. And not least – why do they have a monopoly? 

The pilot must also look at how raw data should be made available. Can the municipalities download their own data? Is it possible to do this directly from UDIR's solution, or is processing needed that makes it more relevant to offer data via, for example, KS Digital's data platform?
