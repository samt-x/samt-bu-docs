## Case description – National service for identifying persons in education (education person register)

### Who submitted this as a case the SAMT-BU project should address

Sikt, based on ongoing work on national registers for education and competence and the need for a shared solution for identifying everyone who takes part in education in Norway.

The case is closely linked to – and can be seen as part of – the work on the national education register and the competence certificate register. Without reliable and unambiguous identification of persons, these registers cannot realise their full potential.

Bergen municipality has also submitted a related need as a case: "Handling of D-numbers and ID changes". Sikt believes that a national service for identifying persons in education could cover this need in a holistic way, and proposes that the two cases be considered together or merged.

### Brief description of the case (what is this / what does it concern?)

The case concerns establishing a national service for identifying all persons who take part in education and training in Norway – from kindergarten and primary and lower secondary school to upper secondary, vocational college, university college and university.

Today the education sector lacks a shared, national solution for identifying and following individuals through their educational pathway. This applies in particular to two issues:

First, the education sector has a large proportion of foreign students, pupils and staff who are not registered in the National Population Register (Folkeregisteret) with a D-number or national identity number. These are currently identified locally by each institution, which is time- and resource-intensive and results in poor data quality.

Second, municipalities and county authorities lack a shared solution for assigning temporary identifiers to children and young people who have not yet received a D-number or national identity number in the National Population Register. Municipalities are obliged to provide services such as kindergarten and school places regardless of whether the child is registered in the National Population Register, and currently solve this by creating fictitious national identity numbers according to their own rules – without coordination across organisations.

The health sector has come further in this area by establishing its own "Auxiliary number" (H-number) and an associated person identification service. Sikt believes the education sector needs a corresponding shared service – an "education person register" – that makes it possible to identify all actors in education unambiguously and across sectors and levels of government.

The investment proposal Sikt has sent to the Ministry of Education and Research for 2027 (ref. 25/00982-1) includes establishing precisely such a national service for identifying persons in education as one of three measures in a holistic national infrastructure for education and competence data.

### What problem does the case address / what is not working well today?

The problem has two parallel dimensions that are closely connected:

Dimension 1 – Foreign students and staff without Norwegian ID:

To realise the full benefit potential of the education register and the competence certificate register, everyone who takes education and courses must have a unique and unambiguous identity. That is not the case today. There is a large proportion of foreign persons in the education sector who are not covered by the National Population Register. Attempts are currently made to identify them locally at each educational institution, which is time- and resource-intensive and does not provide fully satisfactory solutions. The result is that the same persons may exist with different identifiers in different systems, and that data about them cannot be linked across systems.

Dimension 2 – Children and young people without ID in municipal services (the D-number problem):

In the childhood and youth sector there is no shared solution for assigning ID numbers to persons who have not yet received a D-number or national identity number in the National Population Register. As a rule, parents receive a D-number in connection with paying tax, while it may take longer before children receive a D-number or national identity number. In the meantime, municipalities register fictitious national identity numbers according to their own rules – without coordination across municipalities, sectors or levels of government.

The ID number is usually the primary key for person objects in all systems. Without automation or good control, duplicate person objects typically arise in several systems. Fictitious numbers also do not give access to log in to public IT systems via ID-porten.

When a person eventually receives an official ID number in the National Population Register, there is no automated mechanism that links the new number with 100% certainty to existing registrations with a fictitious number. Only similarity in name and address can give an indication, and errors can occur. This can mean that decisions, allocations, history and stored data become inaccessible – and that children and parents lose access they are entitled to.

### Where do breakdowns in information flow or responsibility occur?

Breakdowns in information flow and responsibility occur in particular at these points:

Between the National Population Register and municipal systems: Municipalities are obliged to offer services to children and parents without official ID. When the ID number in the National Population Register is changed or established, there is no automated mechanism ensuring that the municipality's systems are updated accordingly. Matching is at best done by similarity in name and address – a vulnerable and imprecise mechanism.

Between municipalities and across sectors: There is no coordination of fictitious numbers between municipalities or between different sectors. A person may have different fictitious identifiers in the health, childhood and youth, and education systems – without these being linked to one another.

Between kindergarten/school and other childhood and youth services: Children who receive services from child welfare, health or NAV in parallel with schooling may have different identifiers in each system. When one system is updated, the others do not know about the change.

Between educational levels for foreign students: A foreign student who starts upper secondary and then applies for higher education may have different local identifiers in each system – and there is no national service tying them together.

At the transition from D-number to national identity number: Many systems support the transition technically, but it requires active follow-up and manual checking. The systems do not always notify each other, and data may be lost or become inaccessible during the transition phase.

Foreign students may lose access to their digital results when their education is completed and they leave Norway.

### Who are the primary actors affected

End users:

Children and young people who receive municipal services (kindergarten, school, child welfare) without being registered with an official Norwegian ID. Parents and guardians who lose access or lose data about their children in the event of an ID change. Foreign students and pupils who are not registered in the National Population Register. Foreign staff in the education sector without Norwegian ID. Refugees and asylum seekers who have started school or an introduction programme.

Service staff:

Caseworkers and teachers in primary and lower secondary school and kindergarten who must create and maintain fictitious person objects. Counsellors in upper secondary who follow up pupils without official ID. Administrative staff at universities and university colleges who deal with foreign students and staff. IT staff in municipalities and county authorities who maintain integrations with the National Population Register.

Specialist system suppliers:

Suppliers of school and kindergarten administration systems (which currently handle missing-ID solutions very differently). Suppliers of municipal specialist systems for child welfare, health and NAV. Suppliers of student administration systems (FS and similar).

Governing actors:

The Ministry of Education and Research – responsible for the education sector and the investment proposal for national registers. The Norwegian Digitalisation Agency (Digdir) – owner of the National Population Register and ID-porten; governing actor for identity management in the public sector. The Norwegian Tax Administration (Skatteetaten) – administers the National Population Register. The Norwegian Directorate of Health – has established a corresponding solution (Auxiliary number/H-number) and is an important source of experience. KS and KS Digital – the interest organisation for the local government sector; Bergen municipality has already submitted the need as a case. Sikt – technically responsible for national registers for education and competence, and proposer of a national service for person identification in education.

### What are the consequences for the actors under the current situation

For children, pupils and parents:

Children and parents may lose access to data they are entitled to – decisions, allocations, history – in the event of an ID change or a transition from a fictitious to an official number. Parents/guardians lose access to log in to municipal IT systems because fictitious numbers do not give access to ID-porten. Risk that decisions on kindergarten places, special education or other rights cannot be found or linked to the right person.

For foreign students and staff:

The experience of "not existing" in the systems across institutions. Lack of opportunity for continuous documentation of education and competence throughout the course of study. Challenges with access to digital services that require a Norwegian ID.

For municipalities and schools:

Heavy administrative burdens in creating, maintaining and phasing out fictitious person objects. Risk of duplicates and incorrect links in the event of ID changes. Lack of automation means staff spend time on manual checking and error correction. No common standard across municipalities makes it impossible to share data about the same person between sectors.

For national registers:

Without a national service for person identification, the education register and the competence certificate register cannot realise their full potential. Data on education and competence cannot be linked to the right person across levels and institutions, which weakens the value of the entire infrastructure.

### How are actors affected by the current situation, and what are the possible consequences

The consequences are felt at individual level, service level and system level.

For the individual – whether a child in kindergarten, a pupil in upper secondary or a foreign student in higher education – the situation means a real risk of losing data, access and rights in transitions between systems or ID numbers. This is not just impractical; in the worst case it can mean that decisions and rights cannot be traced and implemented.

For municipalities and county authorities, the situation is a daily administrative challenge. Teachers, caseworkers and IT staff spend time solving problems that ought to have been solved at national level. The absence of a common standard means that each system and each municipality has developed its own ways of working, leading to fragmentation and low data quality. When not all pupils in the systems are unambiguously identified, it becomes more difficult to comply with the GDPR, for example in the case of an access request from the data subject, and to ensure that information is not disclosed to the wrong person.

For the national registers for education and competence, this is a structural precondition: without unambiguous identification of all persons in education, it is not possible to build a national infrastructure with high data quality. Data cannot be linked across levels, institutions and sectors.

The health sector has recognised the same problem and solved it by establishing the Auxiliary number (H-number) and an associated person identification service. The experience from this – both benefits and challenges – is well documented and should form the basis for corresponding work in the education sector. Any education person register should be coordinated with the H-number from health so that the same persons do not receive duplicate identifiers across the sectors.

### What might a desired situation (dream journey) look like?

In a desired situation, a national service for identifying all persons in education has been established – an "education person register" – which functions as a shared foundation for all systems in the education sector, from kindergarten to higher education.

The service makes it possible to create a unique, temporary identity for children and young people who have not yet received a D-number or national identity number in the National Population Register. This identity is the same for all sectors – kindergarten, school, child welfare, health – and replaces the many different fictitious numbers that municipalities currently create themselves. The service is aligned with the National Population Register and the health sector's H-number, so that the same person does not receive duplicated identifiers across health and education.

When a person eventually receives an official D-number or national identity number, all connected systems are notified automatically. Data, decisions and history are linked to the new number without manual intervention. Parents and children retain all their access and data through the transition.

For foreign students and staff, the service offers unambiguous identification that enables login across organisations throughout the educational pathway – and that is linked to international identification solutions such as the EU Digital Identity Wallet and the Single Digital Gateway.

The service is a precondition for realising the full potential of the national registers for education and competence. With a shared, quality-assured person identification, data on educational provision, results and qualifications can be linked to the right person across all levels and institutions – giving citizens a combined, lifelong overview of their competence.

### The following insight work has been carried out

The case is based on the following:

Sikt's investment proposal to the Ministry of Education and Research for 2027, appendix 2: "Investment proposal: Further development and operation of national registers for education and competence" (ref. 25/00982-1, dated 31.10.2025). Establishing a national service for identifying persons in education is explicitly described as measure 3 in the investment proposal.

Bergen municipality's input on "Handling of D-numbers and ID changes" (case ID: 6d076c0e-d83d-4590-945b-e8695201138e, last modified 30.03.2026). Bergen municipality describes concretely how the absence of a shared ID solution creates problems in childhood and youth services, and this is a direct basis for this case.

The health sector's experience with the Auxiliary number (H-number) and the person identification service, which is an established parallel in another sector and an important source of learning about both benefits and challenges.

The concept study "Competence platform for lifelong learning" (Report no. 7, 2022, prepared by HK-dir and Sikt), which describes person identification as a precondition for a functioning digital infrastructure around education in a lifelong perspective.

Ongoing work in the inter-agency group for digital transformation in the education sector, where national registers and data sharing are one of four areas of action.

The new national digitalisation strategy, which clearly points to the need for shared identity and access management across sectors.

### Which of the project's objectives do we believe the case addresses?

The case addresses in particular the following SAMT-BU objectives:

Shared information models and standardised interfaces that will eventually cover "all" areas and sectors: A national service for person identification in education is precisely this – a common standard for identifying and following persons that replaces uncoordinated local solutions. This standard will also have to be coordinated with corresponding solutions in health and other sectors, and is thus a contribution to cross-sectoral interoperability.

Reusable solutions and shared solutions: A national identification service is a fundamental shared solution that all systems in the education sector can and should build on. It will reduce the need for sector-isolated ad hoc solutions in individual municipalities and institutions.

Simpler user journeys for staff, parents/guardians, pupils and students through the learning pathway – from kindergarten to higher education: The case directly addresses this objective. With a shared identification service, parents and children will retain their data and access throughout the educational pathway, and staff will no longer have to spend time on manual handling of ID problems.

Scaling and transfer of the project's learning and products to other areas and sectors: The solution will have direct transfer value to other sectors facing the same challenges, and will help strengthen the work on shared identity and access management across the public sector. Coordination with the health sector's H-number is a concrete example of such cross-sectoral scaling.
